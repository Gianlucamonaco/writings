Within the scope of individual disciplines, a transdisciplinary approach might appear unfruitul, as linguistic conflicts and methodological inconsistencies would be unlikely to contribute given the existing boundaries.

Transdisciplinary cannot be reduced to a mere sum of addends, as this would risk creating further boundaries and a fragmenting the object of study. The notion of transdisciplinarity entails the dissolution of boundaries between the disciplines. It means relinquishing the precision defined by the discipline in order to gain a higher level of understanding. In this sense, transdisciplinarity does not present itself as a better substitute for estabilished disciplines; rather, it encourages a constant questioning and renegotiation of the discipline structures, as well as creating space for transformation and the emergence of new fields of inquiry.

**Theory of models**  
I am trying to provide a metaphor to describe how I intend the notion of *model*, and why it might prove useful. Science, philosophy, religion and theories in general can be viewed as structures (or *models*) that we use to understand or explain the world. We *project a model onto the world* we experience. Every *model* is grounded in language (or languages), and it requires a certain degree of approximation and compromise. Every system has boundaries, and axioms that provide the basic components required to build a system that makes sense. Though, it is not possible to explain a system from within. Boundaries can be extended, but they will be set further, never destroyed.

Speaking in geometric terms, each *model* aims to encapsulate the world as effectively as possible, within the constraints of its own structural limitations (basic shapes, rules, etc…)

Unconditional adherence to a single model, seen as the “true” or “only possible” description of reality, can be problematic. Firstly, because a description of reality cannot be identical to reality itself. And secondly, because although a model may work exceptionally well and suit a specific context, its partial effectiveness does not demonstrate its validity in absolute terms.
